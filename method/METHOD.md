# How to do a narrated route walk

This is the method behind the pilot reports in [`../reports/`](../reports/). One person can do it with a smartphone in about 1–2 hours of walking, plus time to write it up.

## 1. Plan the route

- **Start** where participants actually arrive: the nearest rail station, the nearest accessible bus stop, and the drop-off or accessible parking.
- **End** at the front desk of the clinic or research unit, not just the building's main door.
- Check open data before you go, if your city publishes it. Useful sources:
  - curb-ramp and sidewalk inventories
  - 311 sidewalk reports
  - transit elevator status
  - construction permits
  - ClinicalTrials.gov, to see how many studies list the site

  Use what you find to choose which spots to look at closely.

## 2. Walk and narrate

- Record video, holding the phone at chest height, and describe what you see as you go: "the curb ramp here doesn't line up with the crosswalk", "no seat for the last 400 feet".
- Take photos of each barrier. Put an object of known size, or a tape measure, in the frame.
- Use the [checklist](../checklist/) as a prompt, covering:
  - transit stop
  - distance and rest
  - surface
  - crossings
  - drop-off
  - entrance
  - inside
  - senses
  - the day itself
  - cost
- If you can, walk the route a second time at night, in bad weather, or at the hour a study visit would start.

## 3. Respect people and places

- **Don't film people's faces or license plates.** If they end up in a frame anyway, blur them before the images are shared or reviewed by anyone.
- **Don't record inside buildings.** Note indoor observations from memory afterward and mark them as approximate.
- **At care facilities, residential programs and other sensitive sites, observe from the public sidewalk only.** Don't name them in the report.
- The items you record are possible barriers seen by one walker. They are not legal compliance findings.

## 4. Write it up

List every item in a table:

| # | Where | Possible barrier or feature | Observation | Most affected | Confidence | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Confidence:** H = seen and recorded on video or photo; M = seen but not measured, or noted from narration only.
- **Most affected:** e.g., wheelchair, walker, low vision, blind, Deaf or hard of hearing, cognitive, energy-limiting conditions.
- Include **positive features** too: what works is worth keeping and copying.
- Add a short summary, the most important findings with photos, and questions for the site, the city and the transit agency.

## 5. Share and re-check

- Send the report to the hospital's patient and family relations or accessibility office, the research office, the city (311), and the transit agency.
- File separate 311 reports for specific fixable items, such as broken pavement or a missing warning pad.
- **Re-check** after 4–8 weeks and record what was fixed. The fix rate is a simple measure of whether the walk made a difference.

## 6. Ask people with disabilities

One walker sees only part of the picture. Ask people with lived experience what is missing, and update the checklist. See [`../community-input/`](../community-input/) for what has been raised so far.
