# What people with disabilities said, and what changed

The checklist was shared in October 2026 with online communities of people with disabilities and chronic illnesses, along with an anonymous feedback form. This page lists the themes in my own words. It has no names, quotes or group names. The people who commented are sources of information, not partners in the project.

Last updated: 2026-10-30. Form responses so far: 4.

## Themes raised

| Theme | What was raised | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Fares, parking and ride costs are a reason for missing studies, and the real cost is much bigger: lost income and recovery days afterward. | Added to v2 (fares, parking, ride, reimbursement). Lost income and recovery days are planned for v3. |
| **Check-in kiosks** | Touch-screen kiosks can't be used without sight. | Added to v2 ("Inside"). |
| **A place to lie down** | Long waits in hard chairs; nowhere to lie down before or after the visit. Raised often by people with energy-limiting conditions. | Planned for v3 |
| **Waiting time around transit** | When trains run infrequently, people arrive about an hour early and wait as long again to get home. | Planned for v3: schedule visits to match the timetable; count waiting time in the door-to-door total |
| **Standing in lines** | Standing in line at check-in, the pharmacy or the stop is exhausting. | Planned for v3: a seat wherever people wait |
| **Crowding** | Crowded transit and waiting rooms | Planned for v3: crowding at the travel time; offer first or last slots |
| **Ride-hail** | Taxis are expensive. Disabled riders may be charged wait-time fees, and fee waivers are hard to get in practice. | Planned for v3: the site books and pays for rides directly |
| **Crossing time** | A walk signal countdown of about 20 seconds is too short for a manual wheelchair user, someone with a walker, or someone new to a mobility aid. | Planned for the December walks: measure the countdown against crossing width |
| **Companion** | Some people can only make the trip with someone who helps, for example by pushing a wheelchair across a street. | Planned for v3: can a companion come, and does the study cover their cost? |
| **Elevators at transfers** | Several elevators on one trip, signs to them, and whether they work. | Planned for v3 |
| **Restrooms** | Restroom access along the route and at the site | Planned for v3 |
| **Stairs, hills, bumpy surfaces, handholds** | Count stairs and slopes; note rough pavement and a bumpy ride; grab rails and handholds. | Planned for v3 |
| **Senses** | Noise, bright light, crowds | Already in v1 ("Senses"); to be expanded in v3 (a quiet room) |
| **When the trip itself is the barrier** | For some people with energy-limiting conditions, fatigue makes them miss appointments however good the route is. A better route would make no difference for them. | Planned for v3, as a question for study teams: can some visits be done remotely, at home, or as hybrid visits (in person only for procedures)? This is a limit of a route-only approach. |
| **Barriers that make no sense** | A ramp blocked by a railing; recent construction that is still inaccessible | Supports checking new construction, not just old routes |

## Accessibility of the form itself

One person said the form was "easy and quick". That matters for people with energy-limiting conditions. The form will stay short.
