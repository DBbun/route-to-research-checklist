# What people with disabilities said, and what changed

The checklist was shared in October 2026 with online communities of people with disabilities and chronic illnesses, along with an anonymous feedback form. This page lists the themes in my own words. It has no names, quotes or group names. The people who commented are sources of information, not partners in the project.

Last updated: 2026-11-01. Form responses so far: 4. Version 3 of the checklist (two pages: the route, and the visit) answers most of the themes below.

## Themes raised

| Theme | What was raised | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Fares, parking and ride costs are a reason for missing studies, and the real cost is much bigger: lost income and recovery days afterward. | Added to v2 (fares, parking, ride, reimbursement). Lost income and recovery time added to v3 (page 2, Cost). |
| **Check-in kiosks** | Touch-screen kiosks can't be used without sight. | Added to v2 ("Inside"). |
| **A place to lie down** | Long waits in hard chairs; nowhere to lie down before or after the visit. Raised often by people with energy-limiting conditions. | Added to v3 |
| **Waiting time around transit** | When trains run infrequently, people arrive about an hour early and wait as long again to get home. | Added to v3: schedule visits to match the timetable; count waiting time in the door-to-door total |
| **Standing in lines** | Standing in line at check-in, the pharmacy or the stop is exhausting. | Added to v3: a seat wherever people wait |
| **Crowding** | Crowded transit and waiting rooms | Added to v3: crowding at the travel time; offer first or last slots |
| **Ride-hail** | Taxis are expensive. Disabled riders may be charged wait-time fees, and fee waivers are hard to get in practice. | Added to v3: the site books and pays for rides directly |
| **Crossing time** | A walk signal countdown of about 20 seconds is too short for a manual wheelchair user, someone with a walker, or someone new to a mobility aid. | Added to v3 ("time it"); the December walks will measure the countdown against crossing width |
| **Companion** | Some people can only make the trip with someone who helps, for example by pushing a wheelchair across a street. | Added to v3: can a companion come, and does the study cover their cost? |
| **Elevators at transfers** | Several elevators on one trip, signs to them, and whether they work. | Added to v3 |
| **Restrooms** | Restroom access along the route and at the site | Added to v3 |
| **Stairs, hills, bumpy surfaces, handholds** | Count stairs and slopes; note rough pavement and a bumpy ride; grab rails and handholds. | Added to v3 |
| **Senses** | Noise, bright light, crowds | In v1 ("Senses"); v3 adds crowds and a quiet place to wait |
| **When the trip itself is the barrier** | For some people with energy-limiting conditions, fatigue makes them miss appointments however good the route is. A better route would make no difference for them. | Added to v3 (page 2, Fewer trips), as a question for study teams: can some visits be done remotely, at home, or as hybrid visits (in person only for procedures)? This is a limit of a route-only approach. |
| **Barriers that make no sense** | A ramp blocked by a railing; recent construction that is still inaccessible | Supports checking new construction, not just old routes; v3 asks for step-free, signed construction detours |

| **Invisible disability** | People with real limits are told they don't look disabled, or don't qualify for a parking placard. | Added to v3: drop-off for anyone who asks, placard or not; the same help whether or not a disability is visible |
| **Signs at decision points** | The step-free option exists, but nothing points to it where people choose their way. | Added to v3 (page 1, Signs) |
| **Some must drive, others can't** | Driving is the only way for some disabled people; others can't drive at all. | v3 subtitle: the trip by transit, car or ride |
| **Communication access** | Booking and check-in by phone only; sign language interpreters; people who speak with a device, a letter board or pictures. | Added to v3 (page 2, Communication) |

## Accessibility of the form itself

One person said the form was "easy and quick". That matters for people with energy-limiting conditions. The form will stay short.
